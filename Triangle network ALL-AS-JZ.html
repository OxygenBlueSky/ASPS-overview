Figure caption (Combo view)

Network representation of pairwise potency comparisons among the six remedies (Lactose, Stannum, Silicea, Sulphur, Ars. Album, Mercury) under the Combo view: each pair was tested three ways — once on the combined 2016+2022 dataset, and once independently within each year's subset (2016, 2022).

Each of the two outcome measures — Maximum Probability and Root/Sprout Ratio — is drawn as its own line: yellow for Maximum Probability, green for Root/Sprout Ratio. When only one measure is significant for a pair, a single colored line is shown. When both measures are significant, two parallel lines are drawn side by side rather than blending the two into one color — this keeps the two measures' evidence fully separable rather than compressing them into a single visual channel. A pair with neither measure significant is shown as a single plain grey dashed line.

Line thickness reflects the strength of that specific measure's combined-data effect: a thick line denotes that measure reaching p < 0.01 on its own. Because each measure now has its own line, thickness is unambiguous — a thick yellow line always means Maximum Probability itself reached p < 0.01, regardless of what Root/Sprout Ratio is doing.

Line style (solid vs. dashed) is evaluated per measure and reflects whether the two yearly subsets agree on that measure specifically: a solid line means 2016 and 2022 were both independently significant for that measure. A dashed line means the two years disagreed — only one of them reached significance for that measure on its own, even though the combined dataset did. If a measure is significant in the combined data but neither year individually corroborates it, that measure's line is omitted entirely rather than drawn with false confidence — see the next section for exactly which cases this affects.
